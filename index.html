I am a Ph.D. student in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Rice University. I received my B.S. in Computer Science and B.S. in Statistics and Analytics from the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill with Highest Honors. My research builds wearable sensing systems and machine learning methods for biosignals such as EMG, ECG, PPG, IMU and optical myography, with a focus on self-supervised learning for label-efficient gesture recognition and health monitoring. My work has appeared at NeurIPS 2025 (Spotlight), IEEE/ACM CHASE 2026 and IEEE FLEPS 2026.

Research Interests

⌚

Wearable Biosignal Sensing

Building multimodal wearable platforms and end-to-end pipelines for wrist-ECG, EMG, PPG, IMU and high-density optical myography signals.

🧠

Self-Supervised Learning

Designing predictive, generative and reconstruction pretext tasks that let models learn from unlabeled biosignals and adapt with only a few labeled samples.

🤚

Human-Machine Interaction

Robust gesture recognition across days, users and device placement, from sign language recognition to wrist-band interfaces.

🩺

Digital Health & Rehabilitation

Intelligent assistive systems such as smart gloves and soft wearable trackers for post-stroke rehabilitation and long-term physiological monitoring.

Rice

Visiting Scholar

May 2025 - August 2025

Rice University · Supervisor: Dr. Momona Yamagami

Project: Refinement of Gesture Identification Models with One-Shot Fine-Tuning

  • Defined and pretrained a predictive and a generative pretext task on the public Ninapro dataset
  • Predictive task: a CNN-LSTM model recovers the 50% masked "future" data in each sample
  • Generative task: a masked autoencoder (MAE) reconstructs 50% randomly masked data blocks per channel, or 50% unseen channels, reaching a loss of 0.23
  • Performed hyperparameter optimization and one-shot fine-tuning on a locally collected dataset using 10% labeled instances per subject, minimizing data requirements for downstream tasks

UNC

Research Assistant

July 2024 - October 2025

UNC-Chapel Hill · Supervisor: Dr. Wubin Bai

Project: SOMA, Self-Supervised Wearable High-Density Optical Myography for HMI

  • Designed a self-supervised learning pipeline for a 48-channel time-series dataset from a custom NIRS myography sensor array
  • Defined the pretext task as signal reconstruction against electrical noise and random device displacement, using augmentations including random noise, translation, rotation and mirroring
  • Achieved 95% end-task accuracy on the SOMA-ASL (American Sign Language) dataset with only 5 repetitions per gesture, compared with about 70% for the baseline model
  • Showed that pretraining captures motion-invariant features of the sensor data, improving robustness with minimal labeled data

About Me

I am interested in how wearable sensors and machine learning can make health monitoring and human-machine interaction work reliably in everyday life. My undergraduate training in both computer science and statistics at UNC-Chapel Hill gave me a foundation in building systems as well as in reasoning carefully about data.

Across projects on optical myography, wrist-worn electrophysiology, smart rehabilitation gloves and soft laryngeal trackers, I have worked on the full stack: prototyping hardware, building real-time data tools, designing signal processing pipelines and training self-supervised models that need only a handful of labels.

As a Ph.D. student in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Rice University, I continue to explore label-efficient, robust learning for multimodal biosignals. If you share these interests, I would be glad to connect.
